Camera setup matters more than any setting here

Put the phone or laptop on a tripod or a chair, side on to the play, roughly waist height, 6 to 15 feet back, with whole bodies and the floor in frame. Bright even light, no strong backlight. A handheld camera breaks the step counter, because the referee cannot separate your movement from the players'. Calibrating the court removes the perspective problem from step counting, so do it once the camera is placed.

Read this before you trust it

This is a computer vision prototype, not an officiating system. It reads rules from a single 2D camera, so it misses violations and occasionally invents them. Fouls are not detectable this way at all, and the contact flag is a prompt to look, never a judgement. Every call carries a confidence number: treat a low one as a suggestion. Use this for drills and review, not to settle an argument in a real game.
